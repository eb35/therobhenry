History repeats itself. I always **want** to start a personal project using the technology that I **want** to learn. I get excited, I get started, and then I fizzle out. It usually happens due to time constraints and, just like with getting in shape, if I miss one day, it's all over. I also don't want to share a whole lot of personal information on the internet (unless I have to), so I run out of content. That happened in 2021 when I wanted to learn WordPress. I had been wanting to learn how to use it more effectively for a long time and it just happened to match up with my excitement for Docker. It was a valiant effort and I even wrote a handful of articles about it. 

But life happened and time was scarce, the old blog went quiet. I lost the love that I had for WordPress and Docker became just a boring tool that I use at work sometimes. Then inspiration hit me, I fell in love with the [Astro framework](https://astro.build/). I was never a big PHP fanboy and Astro felt like a (nice) step back to the *good ol' days*; just writing HTML, CSS, and JS, with all the modern-day goodies. I never wrote about it, but I watched videos, read articles, followed tutorials, and just loved what Astro was doing. I even paid for a great course made by [Chris Pennington, a.k.a. Coding in Public](https://codinginpublic.dev/). But I never built anything *real* with it.

This time was going to be different 🙄. I knew the framework I wanted to use and I even got more inspiration with falling in love with [Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/). I also wanted to learn how to use AI more effectively to build impressive things with/for me. But "history" started creeping back and I let things rot. I know my numerous readers were saddened.

I can't promise that "history" won't repeat itself again **or** that I won't let this "blog" grow stale while I work on a different project that catches my *fancy*, but my plan now is to take the pressure off of writing (I'm not a writer) and just share simple, short "things" that I find interesting.

So if you're reading this still, "Hello again again (again?)" and enjoy.
